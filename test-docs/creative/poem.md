# The Lantern Keeper

At dusk the harbor folds its silver sails,
And gulls draw commas in the cooling sky.
One lantern wakes beside the weathered rails;
Its amber answer tells the ships where home lies.

The keeper counts no trophies on the wall,
But names the boats that passed through winter rain.
A light need not be large to reach us all;
It only needs to find its flame again.

The moon lays down a path across the bay.
The lighthouse keeps its patient, steady tune.
Some loves are not the words we learn to say,
But windows left alight beneath the moon.

Author: Rowan Vale, a fictional poet.
Form: three four-line stanzas.
Central image: an amber lantern guiding boats home.
Themes: quiet care, persistence, and belonging.
